What the number means

  • It is the size of the day's movement, as a share of the price: the day's volatility, added up from every 5-minute price change. A day that ends higher and a day that ends lower count the same.
  • It says nothing about direction. It cannot tell you to buy or to sell, and it is not a signal.
  • It is for comparing days. A 2% day is twice as lively as a 1% day, so a stop or a position size that suits one does not suit the other.
  • It is not a range or a target. Price can move more than this, and on some days it will.

The record

Every day since 5 November 2026: the forecast beside what happened. "What happened" is the day's real volatility, measured the same way. The plain rule is the common alternative: the average range of the last 14 days.

How it is made, and its limits

  • One fixed model. It was fitted on gold prices up to 2019 and has not been changed since. Its file is checked by its fingerprint () before every forecast.
  • Tested once, on years it had never seen. On 2020 to 2026 its error was 28% lower than the plain rule's, and lower in every single year.
  • The live record is short. A good test on history is not a promise. Judge the record above as it grows.
  • One broker's prices. The forecast is made from an OANDA demo account's gold prices. Another broker's volatility differs a little.
  • Made a few hours into the day. Gold's trading day starts at 18:05 New York time. The note is made the next morning in Europe, before London and New York trade.
  • Some days have no forecast. After a holiday or a shortened session the model has no clean day to start from, and the page says so. The page does not know holidays in advance: on the holiday itself it may still show a forecast, and that day then ends with no result.
  • The same project found no way to predict direction. It tested 21 trading ideas on gold and none passed its test after costs.
